How it works

One router. Several engines. Every doubt shown.

  1. 01

    Detect the script

    From the characters entered, including historical scripts like Modi, Sharada and Grantha.

  2. 02

    Route it

    A neural model for 21 languages, our verified mapping for Modi text, an open vision model for handwritten pages.

  3. 03

    Rank the readings

    Several candidates per word, re-ranked toward real words; stray letters from other scripts are dropped.

  4. 04

    Show doubts, learn

    Unsure words are marked. A correction from a user is remembered and used next time.

What testing taught us

Four findings we only got by measuring.

  1. 01

    A library corrupts श्री

    A standard transliteration library turns र + ी into ॠ in Modi, breaking श्री and क्षत्रिय. We built our own mapping, which round-trips exactly.

  2. 02

    Bengali and Assamese were swapped

    The public model conversion we use had the two language tags swapped. Fixing it lifted Bengali from 9% to 61% and Assamese from 18% to 53%.

  3. 03

    Cleaning scans made them worse

    Denoising and binarising pages raised the error rate from 0.31 to 0.33, since the model learned from raw scans. It did rescue one page where the model looped.

  4. 04

    Flags work best where they're calibrated

    The review threshold was chosen on validation data and confirmed on test data: flagged typed words are wrong 68.5% of the time, and flags catch 42% of mistakes.

Honest limits

What it can't do yet.

  1. —

    Words without context

    "main" can be मैं or में; a word-by-word model can't tell. Alternatives are one click away; sentence-level ranking is next.

  2. —

    Scans need a GPU

    Uploading your own page needs GPU hosting, the next stage. Today's manuscript readings were produced on a GPU.

  3. —

    Corrections need trust

    A correction is used at once only if the model itself proposed it; otherwise two people must agree. Named reviewer accounts for archives come next.
